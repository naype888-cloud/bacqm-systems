Scale-invariant memory dynamics under asymptotic saturation

Abstract

We give an algebraic and operational formulation of hierarchical memory under asymptotic saturation. Deterministic allocation policies treat memory as a static volume; as demand approaches the physical boundary their latency diverges. Here memory is a flow. The elastic domain is split into k collectors of equal precedence, served in turn; a bidirectional transport operator couples it to the fast domain with an exactly closed ledger; and the kernel control law contains no prescription of size, so by the Π-theorem it is a function of dimensionless groups alone and is equivariant under dilation of every capacity. The tail bound that results depends only on ρ/k. Measured on the reference node: tail latency −90 % to −99 % against an unmodified baseline; the fast domain held at ≥ 99.75 % occupancy with no null-state transition; across 425,860 frames of a saturating graphics workload the median frame period stays within 1.04 presentation periods in every occupancy stratum and the p99 stays below 4.9, with no discontinuity at the boundary; and the host processor holds 0.20 mean load while the coprocessor runs at ≥ 90 % duty.

§1Algebraic frame

The system is a hierarchical state space with two volatile domains:

  1. a fast transit domain 𝒱 bounded by C𝒱;
  2. an elastic, compressed domain ℛ of bound Cℛ≫C𝒱.

A conventional kernel projects the working set 𝒮 stationarily, Φ:𝒮→𝒱. When the instantaneous demand σ(t) approaches the bound,

limt→tc ‖σ(t)‖𝒱 =C𝒱−ε,ε→0, (1.1)

a deterministic allocator meets a latency discontinuity: page contention stalls, or the process space is terminated. We restate the problem as conservation of flow. Write a(t) for pages admitted to 𝒱, ei(t) for pages evacuated to collector ℛi and ri(t) for pages returned from it:

σ(t+1)=σ(t)+a(t) −∑i=1kei(t) +∑i=1kri(t). (1.2)

Memory is then an operator of phase density, not a container. Nothing in (1.2) names a size: the algebra is the same for an elementary address space and for a fleet.

Observation 1 · fast-domain boundary · measured

Sustained occupancy ‖σ‖𝒱/C𝒱≥0.9975 without a null-state transition: no panic, no controller reset.

§2Multi-way topology of entropic absorption

A single compression queue is a one-dimensional bottleneck. The elastic domain is split into a bundle of concurrent collectors,

ℛ=⨁i=1kℛi, π(ℛi)=π(ℛj) ∀i,j∈{1,…,k}, (2.1)

where π is the precedence in the routing graph. Equal precedence is a symmetry: the group ℤk permutes the collectors and leaves the dispatcher invariant. The dispatcher interleaves: page n goes to ℛnmodk. There is no serial queue, and evacuation load is spread uniformly as a cyclic tensor.

Let pages leave 𝒱 at rate λ, let each collector absorb at rate μ, and write ρ=λ/μ for the offered load in units of one collector.

Proposition 1 · load split · model

Under (2.1) each collector receives every k-th page, at rate λ/k; its utilization is ρi=ρ/k, the same for all i.

Proposition 2 · tail of the sojourn time · model

With Poisson arrivals and exponential absorption, the sojourn time T in a collector is exponential with rate μ−λ/k. Its q-quantile is

tq·μ= −ln(1−q)1−ρ/k, t0.99·μ=ln1001−ρ/k. (2.2)

Interleaved dispatch makes each collector's arrivals Erlang-k, more regular than Poisson, so (2.2) is an upper bound for k≥2.

Corollary 1 · scale invariance · model

The right side of (2.2) depends only on ρ/k. Under (λ,μ)↦(cλ,cμ) the bound measured in units of 1/μ does not move, and neither C𝒱 nor Cℛ appears in it. The method is orthogonal to the scale.

Corollary 2 · one more lane · model

If ρ≥k the backlog grows without bound. The smallest bundle that restores a finite tail is k*=⌊ρ⌋+1; opening it changes no other parameter.

Realization · deployed

k=2 compressed lanes of equal precedence; the kernel interleaves them. The existing lane is untouched: the second is a path the system takes when it needs it, not a retuning.

§4The transport operator and the closure of the ledger

Equation (1.2) is a balance; a balance is only as good as its ledger. Between the fast domain and the elastic domain the kernel runs a bidirectional transport operator

𝒯:𝒱⇄ℛ, 𝒯(n)= {𝒱→ℛnodd ℛ→𝒱neven, (4.1)

acting on granules of a fixed mass mg read from the running system. The dispatch alternates direction, so (4.1) is the ℤ2 case of the cyclic symmetry of §2. Every request n ends in exactly one of two states, completed or rejected, and both are counted in the same unit.

Definition 1 · ledger

For a window of R requests write R↓,R↑ for the requests toward ℛ and toward 𝒱, N↓,N↑ for those completed and F↓,F↑ for those rejected. The ledger is closed if R•=N•+F• in each direction, to the unit of mass.

Proposition 3 · the residual is the rejected set · model

Under alternating dispatch and a closed ledger, R↓=R↑ for even R, and the net transport is

Δ≔N↓−N↑=F↑−F↓. (4.2)

Subtract the two closure identities and use R↓=R↑.

The operator therefore moves no net mass by itself: any drift of σ through 𝒯 is accounted for, granule by granule, by the requests the fast domain refused.

Observation 3 · exact closure · measured

Cumulative counters of the deployed operator, read at the kernel interface on 24 Oct 2026: R=1260, N=1256 (completion 0.9968), F=4; N↓=630, N↑=626. Requested mass equals completed plus rejected mass exactly, and Δ=4 granules =F↑: all four rejections were admissions into a fast domain at its boundary, as (4.2) requires. Net imbalance: 0.32 % of the transported mass. Evidence E4.

§5Saturation without divergence

Statement (1.1) predicts, for a deterministic allocator, a latency discontinuity as s≔‖σ‖𝒱/C𝒱→1. We test it on a workload that is built to saturate the fast domain: a real-time graphics pipeline whose frame period τf is the latency observable, measured per frame, in units of the presentation period T0 so that the result carries no device scale.

qp(s)≔ 1T0Qp[τf∣s∈Ij], Ij∈{[0,.5),…,[.98,1]}, (5.1)

the conditional p-quantile of the frame period in the occupancy stratum Ij. A latency discontinuity at the boundary means qp(s)→∞ as s→1 for some fixed p.

Observation 4 · bounded at the boundary · measured

Seven runs, 425,860 frames, 23–24 Oct 2026. In every stratum with s≥0.5 (97.3 % of the frames), up to and including s≥0.98 (29,863 frames),

1.01≤q0.5(s)≤1.04, q0.99(s)≤4.85, (5.2)

and the p99 of the top stratum (3.55) lies below that of the stratum beneath it (4.84): the tail does not grow monotonically into the boundary. The p99.9 of the top stratum is 17.3: rare long frames exist at the boundary, and they are finite and counted. The kernel recorded no panic, no out-of-memory action and no guard termination over the seven runs. Below s=0.5 the frames are asset loading, far from the boundary, and that stratum alone carries a heavy tail (q0.99=8.0): the long frames sit where the fast domain is empty, not where it is full. Evidence E3.

Observation 5 · joint saturation · measured

On the 31,222 frames with s≥0.95 and accelerator duty ≥0.90 simultaneously: q0.5=1.32, q0.99=4.98, and 𝔼[loadhost]=0.20. The frame period then follows the accelerator's compute duty, not the memory boundary: Observation 2 at the boundary of the fast domain.

§6Dimensional reduction: the law without prescriptions

Corollary 1 is the queueing face of a more general fact. Call a prescription any constant of the control law that carries a physical dimension and is not read from the state: a byte count, a page count, a size at which the law was tuned. Programming a kernel in the usual sense is writing prescriptions; removing them is the design principle here.

Proposition 4 · Π-reduction (Buckingham, 1914) · model

Let a decision of the control law be a relation f(x1,…,xn)=0 among n state quantities spanning r independent dimensions, with no prescription. Then it is equivalent to

Φ(Π1,…,Πn−r)=0, Π=(σC𝒱, ℓ1Cℛ,…,ℓkCℛ, ρk,P). (6.1)

The dimensional matrix of (xi) has rank r; a relation with no dimensional constant is invariant under the scaling group of the units, and its invariants are generated by n−r monomials of dimension one.

Corollary 3 · dilation equivariance · model

Let the group ℝ>0 act by (C𝒱,Cℛ,σ,ℓi)↦c·(C𝒱,Cℛ,σ,ℓi). Every Π in (6.1) is invariant, hence so is every decision: the trajectory of the law on one address space is, up to dilation, its trajectory on any other. A prescription is exactly what breaks this symmetry, and it fixes the one scale at which the law was tuned: the resonance where a tuned system fails.

Realization · deployed

In the deployed kernel every bound with the dimension of memory is a ratio to a capacity read from the running system: the guard acts at a floor of 3 % of capacity and selects only a process holding ≥ 15 % of it; the transport granule and both capacities are read at load time. The residual prescriptions are of time only (the guard's confirmation and cool-down windows), so Proposition 4 removes the dependence on size completely and leaves the dependence on time to (3.2).

§7Conclusions

  1. Scale is orthogonal to the method. Flow conservation (1.2), the bound (2.2) and the reduced law (6.1) contain no size; they hold unchanged from a single board to a data center.
  2. The balance is exact. The transport operator closes its ledger to the unit of mass, and its only residual is the set of admissions the fast domain refused (4.2).
  3. The boundary is not a wall. Measured at s≥0.98, the frame-period quantiles stay finite and the median does not move (5.2): the discontinuity (1.1) does not occur.
  4. No prescriptions. Feedback read from the state replaces constants of size, and with them the single points of resonance where a tuned system fails (Corollary 3).
  5. Kernel modules can carry the algebra. Low-level optimization decouples from the rigidity of conventional software and approaches the physical limit of the silicon it runs on.
